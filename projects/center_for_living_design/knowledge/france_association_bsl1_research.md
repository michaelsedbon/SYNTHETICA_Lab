# CLD in France — Association Loi 1901 + BSL1 Lab Setup

> Research compiled 2026-03-23. Sources: service-public.gouv.fr, legifrance.gouv.fr, enseignementsup-recherche.gouv.fr, INRS, NIH BMBL guidelines.

## Part 1 — Registering as an Association Loi 1901

### What Is It?

An **Association Loi 1901** is the standard French non-profit structure. It provides legal personality (can open bank accounts, sign contracts, receive grants, employ staff) without the overhead of a company. It's the structure used by La Paillasse, Le Biome, and most French community labs.

### Requirements

| Requirement | Detail |
|------------|--------|
| **Minimum founders** | 2 people (7 in Alsace-Moselle) |
| **Age** | Founders must be ≥ 16 years old |
| **Purpose** | Must be non-profit and lawful |
| **Registered office** | Can be a founder's home, rented space, or maison des associations |
| **Cost** | **Free** — declaration + JOAFE publication have been free since Jan 2020 |
| **Timeline** | Récépissé issued within **5 working days** of filing |

### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Choose a name** — Verify availability on JOAFE and INPI databases
2. **Define the registered office** (siège social) — founder's home, rented space, or communal facility
3. **Draft the statutes** (statuts)
   - Name, purpose (objet social), address, governance rules
   - Conditions for membership, modification, dissolution
   - Tip: keep the objet social broad enough to cover lab activities, art, education, research
4. **Appoint officers** — At minimum a Président; typically also Trésorier + Secrétaire
5. **Hold a constitutive general assembly** (recommended, not mandatory)
   - Approve statutes, elect officers
   - Draft minutes (procès-verbal) signed by the officers
6. **File the declaration** — either:
   - **Online**: via [Service-Public.fr e-creation](https://www.service-public.fr/associations/vosdroits/R1757)
   - **Paper**: Cerfa 13973*04 + Cerfa 13971*04, submitted to the préfecture of the department
7. **Receive récépissé + RNA number** — within 5 days
8. **Automatic JOAFE publication** — free, happens within ~10 days (every Tuesday)
9. **Request SIREN/SIRET** — needed if employing staff, receiving grants, or conducting commercial activities

### Documents Needed

- Signed statutes (PDF, ≤ 1.5 MB if online)
- Procès-verbal of constitutive assembly
- List of officers (name, DOB, profession, address, nationality, role)
- ID copies of officers
- Mandate letter (if filed by a third party)

### Recommended Optional Steps

| Step | Cost | Notes |
|------|------|-------|
| Trademark registration (INPI) | ~€190 | Protect the CLD name/logo |
| Civil liability insurance | ~€100–300/yr | Mandatory for lab activities |
| Bank account | Varies | Dedicated association account |
| Domiciliation service | €20–50/mo | If not using founder's home |

### Key Statutes Clauses for a Lab Association

The statutes should explicitly cover:
- Scientific research and artistic creation as part of the purpose
- Lab safety governance (designate a Biosafety Officer role)
- Membership categories (e.g., resident members, visiting researchers, associate members)
- Access control for lab spaces
- IP and open-source policy for projects

## Part 2 — Operating a BSL1 Laboratory

### What Is BSL1?

**Biosafety Level 1** (BSL1 / Niveau de confinement 1) is the lowest biosafety classification. It covers work with well-characterized agents that **do not cause disease in healthy adults** and present **minimal hazard** to personnel and the environment.

**Typical BSL1 organisms:** *E. coli* K-12, *S. cerevisiae* (baker's yeast), *Bacillus subtilis*, non-pathogenic algae, non-pathogenic fungi (*Pleurotus*, *Physarum*).

### French Regulatory Framework

| Regulation | Scope |
|-----------|-------|
| **Arrêté du 16 juillet 2007** (modified) | Technical containment measures for labs with biological agents |
| **Arrêté du 23 janvier 2013** | Biosafety/biosecurity good practices (micro-organisms & toxins) |
| **Code du travail R. 4421-1 to R. 4427-5** | Worker protection from biological risks |
| **European Directive 2009/41/CE** | Confined use of GMOs (transposed into French Environmental Code) |
| **Code de l'environnement** | GMO regulations (if applicable) |

### BSL1 Facility Requirements

#### Physical Space

| Requirement | BSL1 Specification |
|------------|-------------------|
| **Access control** | Doors that can be locked; restricted access during experiments |
| **Handwashing** | Sink with hands-free operation near the exit |
| **Eye wash station** | Readily accessible |
| **Surfaces** | Benchtops: impervious to water, resistant to chemicals, heat, and solvents |
| **Flooring** | Non-porous, easy to clean (no carpet) |
| **Ventilation** | General ventilation sufficient (no HEPA or negative pressure required) |
| **Windows** | If opening to exterior, must have insect screens |
| **Cleaning access** | Spaces between benches/equipment must be accessible for cleaning |
| **Autoclave** | Must be available (can be on-site or nearby; not necessarily in the lab room) |
| **Waste** | Biowaste decontaminated before disposal (autoclave, chemical disinfection) |

#### Equipment (Minimum)

- Lab coats (worn only in lab, removed before leaving)
- Nitrile/latex gloves
- Safety goggles or face shield
- Mechanical pipetting devices (no mouth pipetting)
- Biohazard waste containers
- Autoclave or pressure cooker for sterilisation
- Disinfectants (70% ethanol, bleach)
- First aid kit

#### Standard Microbiological Practices

- Handwashing after handling materials and before leaving the lab
- No eating, drinking, smoking, or applying cosmetics in the lab
- Decontaminate work surfaces daily and after spills
- All cultures/waste decontaminated before disposal
- Minimise aerosol/splash generation
- Pest control program in place
- Lab-specific safety manual/SOPs

### GMO Regulations — Full Approval Checklist

Since the CLD **will work with GMOs**, the following regulatory process applies. The competent authority is the **Ministère de l'Enseignement Supérieur et de la Recherche (MESR)**.

#### GMO Risk Classes

| Class | Risk Level | Procedure | CLD Relevance |
|-------|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| **Class 1** | Negligible (e.g. *E. coli* K-12 + non-pathogenic inserts) | **Declaration** | ✅ Primary use case |
| **Class 2** | Low | **Authorisation** (CEUCO review) | Possible future |
| **Class 3–4** | Moderate–High | **Authorisation** (strict) | Not applicable |

#### Two-Step Approval Process

GMO work requires **two separate approvals**, both submitted via the national **DUO** (Déclaration d'Utilisation des Organismes) platform:

---

**STEP A — Facility Approval (Agrément d'Installation)**

The physical lab itself must be approved before any GMO declaration can be filed.

| Detail | Info |
|--------|------|
| **What** | Approval that the lab meets C1 confinement requirements |
| **Who issues it** | MESR (Ministry of Research) |
| **Valid for** | **5 years**, for a specific confinement level (C1–C4) and facility type |
| **How to apply** | Download the agrément form from the [DUO platform](https://www.enseignementsup-recherche.gouv.fr/fr/procedures-de-depot-des-dossiers-49498) → email completed form to **labogm@recherche.gouv.fr** |
| **What happens** | MESR's GMO cell reviews the dossier → assigns an **installation number** |
| **Timeline** | Typically **4–8 weeks** from submission of complete dossier |

**Agrément dossier must include:**
- [ ] Description of the facility (floor plan, surfaces, ventilation, waste handling)
- [ ] Confinement level requested (C1 for BSL1)
- [ ] Type of installation (laboratoire)
- [ ] Identity of the responsible person (directeur de laboratoire)
- [ ] Proof that facility meets Annex IV criteria of Directive 2009/41/CE

---

**STEP B — Declaration of Confined Use (Déclaration d'Utilisation Confinée)**

Once the facility has its agrément number, you can declare specific GMO uses.

| Detail | Info |
|--------|------|
| **What** | Declaration of a specific Class 1 GMO project |
| **Who issues it** | MESR |
| **Valid for** | **5 years** |
| **How to file** | Via the [DUO online platform](https://www.enseignementsup-recherche.gouv.fr/fr/procedures-de-depot-des-dossiers-49498) |
| **Outcome** | MESR issues a **récépissé** (acknowledgement) which serves as the approval |
| **Timeline** | **Immediate to 2 weeks** (Class 1 only requires declaration, not authorisation) |

**Declaration dossier must include:**
- [ ] Agrément installation number (from Step A)
- [ ] Identity of the responsible scientist
- [ ] Description of the GMO(s): host organism, vector, insert, source of genetic material
- [ ] Purpose and expected outcomes of the confined use
- [ ] Biological risk assessment (using MESR's self-evaluation template)
- [ ] Confinement and protection measures
- [ ] Waste management protocol
- [ ] Proof of personnel training in biological risks

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Since January 2022**, once a facility has its agrément for Class 1, subsequent new Class 1 uses **no longer require a new declaration** — only a documented risk self-assessment kept on file for inspection. This significantly simplifies ongoing operations.

#### Expert Committee (CEUCO)

The **Comité d'Expertise des Utilisations Confinées d'OGM (CEUCO)** replaced the former HCB scientific committee in January 2022. For Class 1 work, CEUCO review is **not required** (declaration only). For Class 2+, CEUCO evaluates the dossier before MESR grants authorisation.

### Insurance

| Type | Why | Est. Cost |
|------|-----|-----------|
| **Responsabilité Civile (RC)** | Mandatory for the association; covers bodily injury, property damage, intangible damage to third parties | €100–300/yr |
| **RC Exploitation** | Covers day-to-day lab operations | Usually bundled |
| **Multirisque Locaux** | If renting/owning a space — covers fire, water damage, theft | €200–600/yr |
| **RC Employeur** | If employing staff | Bundled with RC |

> [!TIP]
> Work with an insurance broker specialising in associations or biotech. La Paillasse and similar community labs in France may be good reference contacts for finding appropriate insurers.

## Part 3 — Practical Roadmap

### Phase 1: Legal Setup (1–2 weeks)

- [ ] Draft statutes with lab-relevant clauses
- [ ] Identify 2+ founding members and officers
- [ ] Hold constitutive assembly
- [ ] File association declaration online
- [ ] Open dedicated bank account
- [ ] Obtain civil liability insurance

### Phase 2: Lab Space Preparation (1–3 months)

- [ ] Identify and secure a physical space
- [ ] Install BSL1-compliant surfaces, sink, eye wash
- [ ] Acquire basic lab equipment (autoclave, PPE, consumables)
- [ ] Write lab safety manual / SOPs
- [ ] Set up waste disposal system (autoclave + waste collection contract)
- [ ] Designate a Biosafety Officer

### Phase 3: GMO Facility Approval (1–3 months)

- [ ] Download agrément form from [MESR filing procedures page](https://www.enseignementsup-recherche.gouv.fr/fr/procedures-de-depot-des-dossiers-49498)
- [ ] Prepare facility dossier (floor plan, confinement description, responsible person)
- [ ] Email completed agrément application to **labogm@recherche.gouv.fr**
- [ ] Receive installation number from MESR GMO cell (~4–8 weeks)
- [ ] File Class 1 GMO declaration via [DUO platform](https://www.enseignementsup-recherche.gouv.fr/fr/procedures-de-depot-des-dossiers-49498) referencing the installation number
- [ ] Receive récépissé — lab is now approved for Class 1 GMO work
- [ ] Perform self-assessment risk analysis for each new GMO use (keep on file)

### Phase 4: Operations

- [ ] Begin lab activities
- [ ] Maintain training records for all personnel
- [ ] Annual insurance review
- [ ] Document all experiments and safety incidents
